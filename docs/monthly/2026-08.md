## Was August a good month?

Yes — the best month so far, with one clear failure at the end.

The good: highest commit count, the cadence held 27 days straight, and for the first time the work chained properly. A polish pass found two defects, both were fixed inside a week, and `docs/chain-contract-audit.md` (#46) drove the repo's builder-to-builder chain breaks to zero after finding and closing #53. Three starved domains were cleared. Nothing this month depended on a batch re-zip to look busy.

One caveat on that headline, added with a day's hindsight: the 09-01 run falsified the #46 audit's central exclusion. The audit skipped builder-to-reviewer pairs on the premise that a reviewer ships with its builder and so cannot drift from it; three pairs (`traceability-matrix`, `test-case-catalog`, `flexray-config`) are confirmed broken across exactly that boundary, and 43 of 76 reviewers hard-code sheet names. "Chain breaks zero" was true for the surface the audit covered and that surface is under half the repo. August's best artifact needs a re-scope, not a close.

The failure: **the run stopped on 08-27 and did not resume until 08-31.** Three consecutive scheduled runs were lost — Friday's changelog roll (08-28), Saturday's release (08-29), and Sunday's triage (08-30). That is why there is no `v2026.08.W35` tag and no third triage document for the month. July's report already recommended a heartbeat check so a missed run is visible the same day; it was not built, and this time it cost three runs instead of one. This is now the second month running that harness reliability, not engineering capacity, was the binding constraint.

## Recommendations for next month

- **Close the finished issues, then fix why they stayed open.** Nine open issues have shipped fixes and are waiting on a decision no one has made. Add a close step to the polish run — an issue whose DoD is verified in the same commit should be closed by that commit — and make the human taxonomy call on `polish-target` vs. accepting `weekly-target` as the type label. Until that happens the issue count reports the opposite of reality and the 30-day stale rule will keep firing on completed work.
- **Build the heartbeat before anything else in September.** Three lost runs at the end of August is the single largest loss of the month, and it was predicted in the July report. A check that fails loudly when a scheduled day produces no commit is a small piece of work that is now overdue twice.
- **`mbse` and `v&v` get the first slot of September, unconditionally.** Both are at three consecutive zero months. #52 has now been deferred out of two consecutive weekly plans and #58 (mbse) was only opened on 08-31. The August evidence is that pinning a starved domain to the first slot works — `diagnostics`, `sysml`, and `sotif` all cleared that way. Apply it, and do not let a defect found mid-week bump them again. `comms` should take the second slot before it reaches three months too.
- **Start on `quality`.** Thirteen builders, zero commits in August, and six of the ten oldest archives in the repo. It is the largest untouched block in the suite and it will not clear itself from the weekly one-slot rhythm — it needs a deliberate multi-week block, or an explicit written decision that these thirteen are considered stable and exempt from the stale metric.
- **Fix the staleness definition, second ask.** Either credit polish-log and example work as "touched" or keep archive-only, but state the choice in STATUS.md. Two builders got real attention this month and still read red; that makes the watchlist unusable for prioritisation.
